Research

Jeehyun studies atmospheric chemistry and photochemistry across planets, from Solar System atmospheres to exoplanets.

Exoplanet Atmospheric Photochemistry

My research explores how stellar radiation, atmospheric composition, vertical mixing, and temperature structure shape the chemistry of exoplanet atmospheres. I am especially interested in identifying photochemical products that can be observed with JWST and used to infer atmospheric processes beyond simple molecular detection.

Automated Chemical Network Generation

I develop and apply automated reaction network generation methods to build chemical models for planetary atmospheres. By combining tools such as Reaction Mechanism Generator with one-dimensional photochemical models, I aim to construct more complete and chemically traceable reaction networks for diverse planetary environments.

Sulfur Chemistry in Giant Exoplanets

Sulfur-bearing molecules can provide powerful clues about atmospheric metallicity, ultraviolet irradiation, and disequilibrium chemistry. My recent work investigates the photochemical formation of species such as SO2 and CS2 in temperate-to-warm giant exoplanet atmospheres.

Laboratory Kinetics and Planetary Chemistry

My background in chemical kinetics and reaction dynamics informs my approach to planetary atmosphere modeling. I am interested in connecting laboratory measurements, theoretical kinetics, and atmospheric simulations to improve how we interpret chemical signatures in planetary spectra.
